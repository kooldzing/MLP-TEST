Why the Memes Chose Solana

Cheap, fast blockspace is not a neutral utility. It selects for a certain kind of user, and the culture follows the fee.

7 Aug 2026 6 min read

Every network gets the culture its costs deserve. It sounds like a slogan. It behaves like a law.

When a single transaction costs a meaningful amount of money, small experiments become irrational and the network quietly selects for large positions, careful behaviour and people with accountants. When a transaction costs a rounding error and confirms before your attention has wandered, the calculation inverts. You can try things you fully expect to fail. Play becomes affordable, and play is where culture comes from.

The technical shape of the joke

Solana's design decisions happen to be well matched to disposable activity. One global state machine rather than a family of separate rollups, so liquidity and attention pile up in a single place instead of fragmenting across bridges and waiting periods. Confirmation fast enough that trading feels like using an interface rather than instructing a bank. Fees low enough that a failed transaction is an annoyance rather than a financial event you remember.

Critics are right that these properties come with a bill. The validator hardware requirements are demanding, the network has been unstable in the past, and the design concentrates risk in ways a more modular architecture spreads out. Both things are true at once. The point is not that the tradeoffs were correct. It is that they determined who showed up.

A near-death experience is excellent for identity

In late 2022 the exchange most publicly associated with the ecosystem collapsed, and a great deal of the network's reputation went with it. Obituaries were written by serious people. The consensus outside the community was that this particular chain was finished and that its remaining users had not yet noticed.

It was not finished, and the people who stayed through that stretch acquired something no marketing budget can manufacture, which is a shared story about being written off. In-groups form around near-death far more reliably than around success. Much of the tone that outsiders find baffling, the defensiveness, the relentless humour, the specific relish about having been underestimated, is a direct inheritance from that period. Culture is downstream of memory, and that memory is recent enough to still be doing work.

Communities do not bond over winning. They bond over the stretch when everyone assumed they were already dead.

When issuing a token became a way of posting

Historically, creating a token was an engineering project. Write code, deploy it, seed a market, persuade someone to list it. Each step filtered people out, and the filter was doing quiet work by ensuring that anybody who reached the end had at least invested effort. Over the last cycle that entire pipeline collapsed into a handful of clicks, and the filter went with it.

What emerged is genuinely new, whatever you think of it. Issuance became a communication medium. A token is now a way of saying something, a punchline or a tribute or a reaction to the news, that happens to arrive with a live price attached. The overwhelming majority say nothing worth hearing, exactly as with any medium where posting is free. The interesting consequence is not the tokens themselves. It is that a market price got bolted onto a joke, so the joke acquires a scoreboard, and having a scoreboard changes how people tell jokes.

The uncomfortable half of the ledger

Honest accounting requires saying that the same properties producing the creativity also produce the extraction. Nearly costless issuance guarantees an endless supply of things designed to separate people from their money. Speed that makes trading feel like a game means it is a game played with real losses. In a market this reflexive, most participants lose, and those with better tooling and earlier information lose considerably less often than everyone else.

The ecosystem is not unaware of this. The argument between consumer applications and a casino with a good roadmap is an internal one, conducted loudly and in public, and it is nowhere near resolved. Anyone presenting either side as the settled truth is selling something.

What is actually being tested

Underneath the noise there is a real experiment running, which is what happens to human behaviour when the cost of coordinating money falls close to zero. Meme coins are the most visible and least dignified answer, but they are also a stress test of exactly the rails that would carry payments, ticketing, credentials and ownership records for dull physical things. The frivolous use case arrives first because it is cheap to build, needs no permission and does not have to ask a compliance department anything.

A chain does not decide what it is for. It makes certain behaviours cheap and others expensive, and people supply the rest. Solana made expression close to free and received the culture that near-free expression produces, with everything admirable and everything grubby that this implies.
